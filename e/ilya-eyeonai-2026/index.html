本期速览 · Overview

OpenAI 联合创始人 Ilya Sutskever 回顾从 2003 年机器学习起步，到坚信大规模深度神经网络在足够数据下必然成功的历程。

OpenAI co-founder Ilya Sutskever traces his path from 2003 machine learning to the conviction that large deep neural networks trained on enough data will necessarily succeed.

要点 · TL;DR

  • 缩放只有在缩放正确的东西时才有效；深度学习是第一个有效利用规模的方法。
    Scaling works only when you scale the right thing; deep learning is the first productive way to use scale.
  • 足够好地预测下一个词就能解决无监督学习并获得对世界的理解。
    Predicting the next word well enough solves unsupervised learning and yields world understanding.
  • 幻觉很可能可以通过改进基于 RLHF 的后训练来修复。
    Hallucinations can likely be fixed by improving post-training with RLHF.

核心观点 · Key points

  • 深度学习首次让规模扩张变得高效；关键在于扩张什么。
    Deep learning is the first productive way to use scale; what you scale matters.
  • 足够好的下一个词预测解决了无监督学习，并带来对世界的理解。
    Predicting the next word well enough solves unsupervised learning and yields world understanding.
  • 幻觉很可能可以通过改进基于人类反馈的强化学习（RLHF）后训练来修复。
    Hallucinations can likely be fixed by improving post-training with RLHF.
  • 多模态有用但非必需；仅凭文本也能教会颜色等概念。
    Multimodality is useful but not necessary; text alone can teach concepts like color.
  • 预训练模型已经编码了真实世界过程的压缩知识。
    Pre-trained models already encode compressed knowledge of real-world processes.
  • 从更少数据中更快学习将解锁更可靠、更可控的模型。
    Faster learning from less data will unlock more reliable and controllable models.

反共识 · Contrarian takes

  • 《苦涩的教训》夸大了其主张：必须扩张特定的东西，而非仅仅扩张。
    The bitter lesson overstates its case: you must scale something specific, not just scale.
  • 学习统计规律远比多数人想象的更重要。
    Statistical regularity learning is a far bigger deal than most people think.
  • 当前自回归Transformer已能很好地处理高维不确定预测。
    Current autoregressive transformers already handle high-dimensional uncertain predictions fine.
  • 心理学的语言现在可能适合用来理解神经网络的行为。
    Psychology's language may now be appropriate to understand neural network behavior.
  • 高带宽的民主过程可能让公民塑造神经网络的行为方式。
    A high-bandwidth democratic process could let citizens shape how neural nets behave.

That was the result of the technical work that was done in Jeff's lab. So you combine the two. We can train those neural networks. It needs to be big enough so that if you trained it, it would work well and you need the data which can specify the solution. And with ImageNet all the ingredients were there. GPT与Transformer起源 Origins of GPT and Transformers

Host

GPT 项目是在什么时候启动的？当时对 Transformer 和自监督学习有什么直觉吗？能谈谈吗？

At what point did the GPT project start? Was there some intuition about transformers and self-supervised learning? Can you talk about that?

Ilya

背景是这样的：在 OpenAI 的最早期，我们就在探索“预测下一个东西就是你所需要的一切”这个想法。当时我们用受限得多的神经网络来探索。但希望在于，如果你有一个神经网络能预测下一个词、下一个像素，那本质上就是压缩。预测就是压缩。而预测下一个词并不是——让我想想怎么解释最好，因为当时有很多事情在同时进行，它们都相关。也许我换个角度说。我们确实很感兴趣的是，预测下一个词能走多远，它是否能解决无监督学习。所以在 GPT 之前，无监督学习被认为是机器学习的圣杯。现在它已经被完全解决了，甚至没人再谈论它，但它曾经是圣杯，非常神秘。所以我们一直在探索这个想法。我当时非常兴奋，认为只要预测下一个词足够好，就能实现无监督学习。如果它能学到数据集的一切，那就太好了，但我们的神经网络当时无法胜任。我们用的是循环神经网络。当 Transformer 出现时， literally 论文一出来， literally 第二天，我就清楚地意识到，Transformer 解决了循环神经网络在学习长期依赖上的局限。这是个技术问题，但我们立刻就转向了 Transformer。于是下一个 GPT 项目继续推进，有了 Transformer 它开始工作得更好，你把它做大，然后我们意识到需要不断做大，我们确实这么做了，最终导致了 GPT-3 和今天的局面。

So for context, at OpenAI from the earliest days, we were exploring the idea that predicting the next thing is all you need. We were exploring it with the much more limited neural networks of the time. But the hope was that if you have a neural network that can predict the next word, the next pixel, really it's about compression. Prediction is compression. And predicting the next word is not—let's see, let me think about the best way to explain it because there were many things going on. They were all related. Maybe I'll take a different direction. We were indeed interested in trying to understand how far predicting the next word is going to go and whether it will solve unsupervised learning. So back before the GPTs, unsupervised learning was considered to be the holy grail of machine learning. Now it's just been fully solved and no one even talks about it, but it was a holy grail. It was very mysterious. And so we were exploring the idea. I was really excited about it—that predicting the next word well enough is going to give you unsupervised learning. If it learns everything about the data set, that's going to be great, but our neural networks were not up for the task. We were using recurrent neural networks. When the transformer came out, it was literally as soon as the paper came out, literally the next day, it was clear to me, to us, that transformers address the limitations of recurrent neural networks of learning long-term dependencies. It's a technical thing, but it was like we switched to transformers right away. And so the very next GPT effort continued then, and with the transformer it started to work better, and you make it bigger, and then we realized we need to keep making it bigger, and we did, and that's what led to eventually GPT-3 and essentially where we are today.

Ilya

不，我会说，当他发表那篇文章时，我们很高兴看到一些外部人士有类似的思路，我们认为它表达得非常雄辩。但我实际上认为，我所阐述的“苦涩的教训”夸大了它的论点，或者至少我认为人们从中得出的结论夸大了它的论点。人们得出的结论是：你做什么不重要，只要扩大规模。但这并不完全正确。你必须扩大某个具体的东西。你必须有一个能够从规模中受益的东西。深度学习的伟大突破在于，它为我们提供了第一种有效利用规模并从中获得回报的方式。在那之前，人们会用大型计算机集群做什么？我想他们会做天气模拟或物理模拟之类的，但也就这样了。也许拍电影，但没有人真正需要计算机集群，因为你用它做什么呢？深度神经网络，当你把它们做大并用更多数据训练时，效果更好，这为我们提供了第一个值得扩展的东西。但也许有一天我们会发现，我们所扩展的东西上有一个小转折，会使得扩展效果更好。现在这个转折有多大——当然事后诸葛亮你会说这算什么呢，这么简单的改变——但我认为正确的说法是，你扩展什么很重要。现在我们只是找到了一个可以扩展的东西，它能给我们带来回报。

No, I would say that when he posted his article, we were very pleased to see some external people thinking in similar lines and we thought it was very eloquently articulated. But I actually think that the bitter lesson as I articulated overstates its case, or at least I think the takeaway that people have taken from it overstates its case. The takeaway that people have is: doesn't matter what you do, just scale. But that's not exactly true. You got to scale something specific. You got to have something that will be able to benefit from the scale. The great breakthrough of deep learning is that it provides us with the first ever way of productively using scale and getting something out of it in return. Like before that, what would people use large computer clusters for? I guess they would do it for weather simulations or physics simulations or something, but that's about it. Maybe movie making, but no one had any real need for computer clusters because what do you do with them? The fact that deep neural networks, when you make them larger and you train them on more data, work better provided us with the first thing that is interesting to scale. But perhaps one day we will discover that there is some little twist on the thing that we scale that's going to be even better to scale. Now how big of a twist—and then of course with the benefit of hindsight you will say does it even count, it's such a simple change—but I think the true statement is that it matters what you scale. Right now we just found a thing to scale that gives us something in return.

大语言模型的局限 Limitations of Large Language Models
