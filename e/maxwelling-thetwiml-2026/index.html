大脑中的波与神经网络 Waves in the Brain and Neural Networks

Max

现在在大脑中确实观察到了波，因为我们从单电极测量发展到了数百或数千个电极的测量。当然，在单个电极中，你无法看到移动的波，即行波。但如果你放入数百或数千个电极，你实际上可以看到这些行波。现在人们已经在各处观察到了它们。在神经网络中，一直有点难以确保信息从输入层一直传播到输出层，穿过数千层。所以问题变成了：你如何深入时间进行通信或深入时间进行推理，或者如何与你大脑中非常遥远的事物进行通信？因此问题变成了：我们能否真正开始利用这种现象，这种我们在大脑中看到的波现象，并且我们能否也开始在神经网络中使用它？这就是我们一直在开发的工作。

Waves are actually seen in the brain now because we've gone from single electrode measurements to maybe hundreds or thousands of electrode measurements. And of course, in a single electrode, you cannot see a moving wave, a traveling wave. But if you put hundreds or thousands in them, you can actually see these traveling waves. And people have now observed them everywhere. In neural networks, it's always been a little hard to make sure that the information travels all the way from the input to the output layers through thousands of layers. So the question becomes: how do you communicate very deep into time or reason very deep into time, or how do you communicate with things that are very far away in your brain? And so the question becomes: can we actually start using this phenomenon, this wave phenomenon that we see in the brain, and can we also start to use it in neural networks? And so this has been the work that we've been developing.

Yeah, so this is very exciting. There are two reasons why we thought we needed to think more about waves in science. First of all, waves are actually seen in the brain now, because we've gone from single-electrode measurements to maybe hundreds or thousands of electrode measurements. The other reason is that in neural networks you have this phenomenon called over-smoothing, which means that you start with a piece of information and you have thousands of layers, and this information is basically exponentially suppressed as you go through it. That's a very good point. If you think of this neural network as a dynamical system, you basically think of the layers as time, and you're trying to get a signal from the beginning and propagate it through the layers over time. So a couple of things can happen. There are different types of dynamical systems. The first one is a stable system where, basically, if you take two different inputs, they map to—after a while they collapse onto the same point, and then from there on that one point moves forward. So basically everything converges onto what's called a fixed point, and then from there on that's what propagates. That's not what you want, because information gets lost because everything gets mapped to a point. That's an inloating gradient, if you wish. That's something where the information gets lost. There's another extreme where you take two input points—say two images that are similar—and then they start to wildly move around, and that's called chaos. So if it's a nonlinear system, it starts to wildly move around, and you also lose the information, but for a different reason. The information is still there, but you cannot track it numerically.
