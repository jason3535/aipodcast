本期速览 · Overview

在软件易建的时代，Julian Martin 揭示细微交互与创造文化如何让产品真正脱颖而出。

In an era when software is easy to build, Julian Martin reveals how small interactions and a creative culture make products truly stand out.

要点 · TL;DR

  • 令人愉悦的微交互，而非纯粹的功能，让设计令人难忘，并成为战略选择。
    Delightful micro-interactions, not pure function, make design memorable and are a strategic choice.
  • 在细节上投入能让产品比竞争对手高出 2-3%，正如 Ammo 独特的视觉识别所展示的那样。
    Investing in details raises a product 2-3% above competitors, as shown by Ammo's distinctive visual identity.
  • 副业项目比学历背景更能体现好奇心；小团队和跨部门协作驱动顶尖初创公司的创意。
    Side projects signal curiosity better than pedigree; tiny teams and cross-collaboration drive top startup ideas.

核心观点 · Key points

  • 愉悦感来自许多微小的、经过思考的交互——而不是撒花或徽章——这能创造出用户喜爱的独特体验。
    Delight comes from many tiny, thoughtful interactions—not from confetti or badges—and creates a unique experience users love.
  • 视觉工艺与精确对齐是基础；在细节上反复迭代，才能让特别的产品与普通产品拉开差距。
    Visual craft and precise alignment are essential foundations; iterating relentlessly on details separates special products from ordinary ones.
  • 借用被遗忘的物理交互模式，比如用滚轮来浏览好友，会让交互更有触感、更令人难忘。
    Designing with forgotten physical patterns, like a rolodex wheel for friends, can make interactions feel more tactile and memorable.
  • 强有力的协作意味着设计师和工程师并肩工作、实时磨合，而不是单向交接和各自为政。
    Strong collaboration means designers and engineers working side by side, jamming in real time, rather than handoffs and isolated work.
  • 对于当下精益创业团队，副业项目和全方位技能——设计、代码、AI 能力——是最强招聘信号。
    Side projects and a 360 skill set—design, code, AI fluency—are the strongest signals when hiring for today's lean startups.

反共识 · Contrarian takes

  • 不是每个界面都必须“实用”；愉悦和趣味本身就可以是产品的意义。
    Not every screen needs to be functional; delight and playfulness can themselves be the point of a product.
  • 从零创造时，数据不够用——直觉和本能必须领路。
    When creating something from scratch, data is not enough—gut feeling and intuition must lead the way.
  • 大厂背景在创业公司里常常是减分项；副业作品和创始人般的饥饿感更重要。
    Big-company backgrounds are often a liability at startups; side projects and founder-like hunger matter more.
  • 追求人数是过时的成功指标；七个人的小团队也能比大组织产出更多。
    Chasing headcount is an outdated success metric; a tiny team of seven can outbuild large organizations.
  • 多花两天打磨，好过快速上线；“先发了再说”的旧心态已经失效。
    Spending two extra days on polish is better than shipping fast; the old 'just ship it' mindset no longer works.
  • 设计系统有时会阻碍重塑；你必须推倒一切，从白纸开始。
    A design system can hold back reinvention; sometimes you have to discard everything and start with a blank page.

设计差异化与愉悦 Design Differentiation and Delight

Host

随着软件越来越容易创造，真正靠设计做出差异化需要什么？

As software becomes easier to create, what does it take to truly differentiate with design?

Julien

如果你使用这个应用时会心一笑，即使过了 45 天，你也会知道你为用户创造了真正特别的东西。关键在于那些小细节。比如在 Ammo 上，在 Bump 上，当你浏览好友时。你不必把一切都做成功能性，它也可以很好用。

If you're smiling when you use this app, even after the 45 days, you know you created something really special with users. It's all the little things. For example, on Ammo, on Bump, when you browse through your friends. You don't have to build everything to be functional. It can be great to use it.

Host

作为设计师，你如何营造创意文化，并持续跳出固有框架思考？

How do you form a culture of creativity and consistently think outside the box as a designer?

Julien

不需要有五彩纸屑，也不需要到处是蓝色或饱和的色彩。但如果你总在想：嘿，我点这个按钮会发生什么？反应是什么？反馈是什么？也许只是一个微小的动画，也许是手机上精心设计的触感反馈，也许是屏幕上发生的其他事情。正是这类交互和愉悦感，才会创造独特的体验。

It doesn't have to have confetti. It doesn't have to have blue colors or saturated colors everywhere. But if you always think about, hey, what happens when I tap this button? What's the reaction? What's the feedback? Perhaps it's just a tiny animation. Perhaps it's haptic feedback that is really carefully done on your phone. Perhaps it's something else happening on the screen. But it's this type of interactions and delight that will create a unique experience.

Julien

当我讲起这个故事，尤其是最近在 Zenly 和 Ammo 的时候，大家就是不相信我。我们几乎把它当成一个内部笑话来调侃——人们会讨论我当时是不是真在撒谎。那是很久以前了，2006 年。我来自法国东部一个叫斯特拉斯堡的城市。那是一座美丽的城市，充满历史和漂亮的建筑，融合了德国和法国的文化。那里没有任何工作机会，尤其是设计领域。我只有一点点平面设计方面的糟糕经验，甚至算不上网页设计。后来我一个朋友在纽约开了家餐厅，他邀请我过去，就当度一个三个月的长假。我当时年轻又有点傻——其实那时我 22 岁，不，24 岁，抱歉——我去了之后就在他的餐厅帮忙。我后来还成了一家餐厅的经理。事情是这样的：我决定留下来，以游客身份留在美国。现在我可以讲这些了，因为我不觉得丢人，但它确实与今天的世界政治、美国政治产生某种共鸣：我当时逾期滞留了。我不想回法国。有趣的是，我是非法滞留，但我纳税了。在这家餐厅，我下班后总是看到一位顾客下午来，安静地坐在角落里，在他的笔记本——Moleskine 本子上画东西。他每天下午同一时间来，待同样长的时间，点同样的东西：一份华夫饼和一杯双份浓缩咖啡。你能看出来——人们通常只点一杯单份浓缩或双份浓缩，这确实是法国人或欧洲人的习惯。就是简单、浓烈的咖啡，仅此而已。所以我猜这个人不是本地人。结果，他来自西班牙巴塞罗那，他正在同一条街上创办一家新公司，而那家餐厅就在这条街上。这家公司叫 Behance。

When I tell the story, especially recently, you know, at Zenly and Ammo, people just don't believe me. We kind of made fun of this as almost an inside joke that people were discussing whether or not I was actually lying about this back then. It's a long time ago. It was 2006. I'm from a city in eastern France called Strasbourg. It's a beautiful city, packed with history and beautiful architecture. There's a lot of culture there, both German and French. I brought some pastries and stuff to say thank you. The office was just an apartment—Scott Belsky's apartment. It's really funny—you can tell by the look, the smell, by everything that something was going on in there. It was a beautiful space, a very New York apartment. There were many huge paper sheets plastered on the walls and even the windows, so it was blocking the light a little bit. Many screens.
